# Phase 0: foundation

Done. This is what shipped, where it departs from the plan, and what Phase 1
inherits.

## Acceptance

> Done when: you can query effective sets for a hand-inserted week and the number
> is right.

`npm test` runs the Vitest integration suite. The Phase 0 coverage lives in
`tests/integration/stats.test.ts`: it inserts a known week against a throwaway
user, asks the database for effective sets, and compares the result to numbers
worked out by hand. It asserts four things:

1. Effective sets sum per-muscle factors rather than counting sets.
2. Warm-up sets contribute nothing.
3. The trailing window excludes older work.
4. Tonnage weights by the same factor as effective sets.

All pass. The fixture user is deleted afterwards, so it is safe to run against
the real database.

## What shipped

- Sixteen tables on Neon, two migrations, all indexes from the plan.
- Better Auth with email and password, wired through the Drizzle adapter.
- 873 exercises seeded from `yuhonas/free-exercise-db`, with 272 hand-corrected.
- Front and back body SVGs, all eighteen regions, generated and verified against
  the muscle list.
- oRPC set up on day one, as the plan asks, with `stats` and `catalogue` routers.

## Departures from the plan

**`sessions` is named `workout_sessions`.** Better Auth owns a `session` table.
Two tables one character apart is a bug waiting to be written.

**No OAuth provider.** Deferred past MVP at your call. The `account` table
already holds everything a provider needs, so adding one is config, not schema.

**`unit_pref` is a text column with a check constraint, not an enum.** Better
Auth's additional fields are always text, so an enum type could not be used.

**`exercises` gained four columns:** `source_id`, `category`, `mechanic` and
`instructions`. `source_id` is what makes reseeding idempotent and lets a tagging
correction be traced back. The other three were free in the source data and are
useful in the picker.

**Timestamps are `timestamptz` everywhere,** including Better Auth's tables. The
generated schema used naive `timestamp`, which postgres.js round-trips through
the process-local zone.

**`account.issuer` is maintained by hand.** The published Better Auth CLI is
1.4.21 and deprecated; the library is 1.7.1 and added `issuer` plus a unique
index on `(issuer, account_id)`. Regenerating the auth schema will drop both —
re-add them.

## The tagging problem, honestly

The plan calls bad muscle factors the thing most likely to sink this, and it is
right. The source's vocabulary is seventeen coarse tags against our eighteen
regions, and it is lossy in ways that matter:

- **`shoulders` is a single tag** covering front, side and rear delts, which are
  three different training decisions. Un-overridden, it splits half a set to
  front and half to side delts, so a set is never double-counted but the split is
  a guess.
- **`abductors` and `neck` have no region.** They fold into glutes and traps.
- **Several source tags are simply wrong.** It calls the powerlifting bench a
  triceps exercise, the deadlift a lower-back exercise, and cable hip adduction a
  quad exercise.

272 exercises are corrected by hand in `lib/exercise-seed/overrides.ts`,
covering the compounds, the delt work, the arm and leg isolation people actually
run, and the outright errors above. Every override key was checked against a real
source id, so none silently do nothing.

**This is short of the plan's "get the top 150 right".** The 272 cover far more
than 150 rows, but they were tagged from training knowledge, not verified against
your own logs. The plan's real instruction — "verify a few weeks of your own
training against what the SVG shows" — has not happened and cannot until Phase 1
exists. Do that before trusting the heatmap in Phase 3.

The long tail beyond the overrides is rough by design. `movement_pattern` is left
null for anything not hand-tagged, because guessing it from an exercise name
produces confident nonsense.

## The body SVGs

Generated by `scripts/generate-body-svg.ts` into `public/` and, for inlining,
`lib/body-svg.generated.ts`. Each region is a `<g>` carrying the muscle's
`svgPathId`, holding a left and a right copy.

The figure is laid out on an eight-head canon against a table of named vertical
landmarks, and the muscle layer is clipped to the silhouette. Four things were
worth fixing and are worth not undoing:

- **Curve tension is 0.6, not the textbook 1.0.** At full tension Catmull-Rom
  overshoots corners, and the pectoral bulged over the deltoid while the
  trapezius spilled onto the chest. Regions that are adjacent in the coordinates
  were rendering as overlapping.
- **Any region whose medial edge is the spine or sternum needs points along the
  centre line**, not just the two endpoints. Without them the closing curve bows
  inward and the shape renders as a blade. That is what turned the trapezius
  into a wedge across the lats.
- **Silhouette opacity is set on the group, not per path.** The body is a union
  of overlapping parts, so per-path alpha composited a darker seam across the
  shoulders, armpits and hips.
- **The arm is held away from the ribs.** Flush against the torso, the biceps
  sits on the obliques and the whole side of the body reads as one mass.

Regions are drawn to sit beside each other rather than on top. Overlap
composites into a darker band that the eye reads as a fifth volume level, which
would make the heatmap lie.

They are still diagrams rather than anatomical plates. They are correct where it
counts: every id matches the muscle list, the generator exits non-zero if a
region is missing from the view its `bodySide` claims, and `BodyMap` namespaces
both ids and `url(#…)` references per view because front and back legitimately
share four regions and each defines its own clip path. A bought illustration can
replace them by keeping the ids, with no code change.
